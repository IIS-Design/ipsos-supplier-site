01 · Executive summary

Do not copy seven roles before understanding their three purposes

The references provide valuable context about the existing system. They are not, by themselves, a validated contract for the new portal: they mix permissions, email distribution and organizational job roles, and include inconsistent claims.

Recommendation to validate: an intermediate approach

Provisionally retain Administrator/User access. Explore recipients grouped into operational and billing communications, separately from team-administration or cost-approval authority. Preserve multiple recipients and external contacts while validating actual usage. Do not change cost authorization, migrate accounts or introduce child suppliers on the basis of this report.

Already implemented

Focused UI improvements

MIR setup, clearer Redirects/S2S configuration, contextual help, validation and in-memory saved overrides. Real integration remains outside the prototype.

Explore without activating

Separate the concepts

Access permission, operational responsibility and communication recipients. Show who would receive an event without changing actual distribution yet.

Decisions required

Authority and commitments

Who manages the team, who approves, what cost confirmation means, which communications are necessary and how existing accounts are preserved.

03 · Proposed intermediate approach

A clearer experience without recreating the organization chart

A design proposal for discussion. It is neither implemented nor approved as a new flow. An event matrix and permission decisions are required before changing real recipients or access.

Use supplier defaults / Custom recipients

Fictional examples. Being a recipient does not grant access or approval authority. Multiple recipients and non-login emails remain an option to validate.

What proposing this would involve

  1. Validate the grouping: establish whether Project/Billing covers events without mixing messages or documents that need different distribution.
  2. Extend the contact model: introduce categories and scope. Renaming Default Contacts is not sufficient.
  3. Define overrides: decide whether a custom list replaces every group or one group. No new inheritance rule has been approved.
  4. Confirm compatibility: establish how recipient groups map to existing system resolvers. A new design may not be operable without that agreement.
  5. Start with visual validation: use fictional data and scenarios to assess comprehension. Do not connect real sending or activate new permissions at this stage.

Not included: seven new roles, subscriptions for every event, multi-account access, automatic TO/CC changes, new approvers or account migration.

04 · Ideal direction in stages

From intent to a visible outcome

  1. The organization defines who may do whatAccess and capabilities reflect real actions. Job titles do not implicitly grant authority.
  2. It configures recipients around the workField updates, billing and additional groups only where justified. Users and external emails follow validated rules.
  3. The subsample shows effective configurationShow an operational owner if one is needed, separately from recipients. Distinguish inherited and custom settings and identify who may edit them.
  4. The effect is understandable before savingShow who will receive communications, where configuration comes from and what is missing. Flag missing coverage where an event requires recipients.
  5. Cost decisions have explicit meaningPublishing, requesting review and confirming are distinct. Agree CPI-locking and bulk-eligibility requirements before implementing them.
  6. The outcome can be inspectedRecord actor and time for relevant decisions, and trace permission/contact changes where required. Delivery visibility follows the actual contract, not a promise of guaranteed delivery.

Redirect signing, retries, a Content Type editor, testers and other internal administration capabilities do not automatically become supplier features simply because they belong to an ideal future architecture.

12 review points available

01Multiple roles per contactModel to validate · Impact: High

Current situation and evidence

The prototype assigns one access role: Administrator or User. The documents describe seven roles that can be combined on a single supplier contact.

Reference: S3 · S4 · P1. See the sources section.

What to challenge

The catalogue mixes portal authority, communication responsibilities and organizational labels. Reproducing it would carry that ambiguity into the new UI.

Intermediate approach

Keep Administrator/User provisionally. Explore separate recipients for operational and billing communications. Do not turn job titles into permissions automatically.

Ideal direction

A small set of action-based capabilities and event-appropriate recipients. Job titles may remain optional information or a compatibility layer if there is a demonstrated need.

What changing this would involve

Multiple roles would affect profiles, invitations, session handling, navigation, API authorization, data and tests. A UI-only separation is insufficient without an agreed integration contract.

Questions to resolve

  • Which role combinations are actually used, and what problem do they solve?
  • Are all seven roles supplier needs, or mainly a historical email-distribution structure?
Pending validation. No implementation authorized.
02Project Director and team administrationPermission to validate · Impact: Medium / high

Current situation and evidence

Administrator manages users today. Both HTML references also allow Project Director to manage the team.

Reference: S3 · S4 · P1. See the sources section.

What to challenge

A project job title may be granting authority over users across the organization. The available evidence does not justify extending that access to every Settings section.

Intermediate approach

Retain current access. Establish whether Directors need to edit portal users, project contacts, or both.

Ideal direction

A Manage team capability independent of job title, only if delegation is a real requirement. Avoid introducing permissions for every button without a use case.

What changing this would involve

Permission changes require matching UI, API and repository controls, plus safeguards that keep account administration available.

Questions to resolve

  • Does the Director administer the entire supplier account or only project contacts?
  • Can a Director appoint Administrators or grant capabilities they do not hold?
Pending validation. No implementation authorized.
03One default recipient per roleLegacy rule to challenge · Impact: High

Current situation and evidence

Default Contacts supports multiple recipients, including email addresses without a portal account. The references describe one default contact for each role.

Reference: S3 · S4 · P2. See the sources section.

What to challenge

A single person per role may complicate coverage, shifts and shared mailboxes. Defaults for roles with no documented active use are also difficult to explain.

Intermediate approach

Explore recipient groups by communication type while preserving multiple recipients and external email addresses. Group cardinality and scope still need approval.

Ideal direction

Recipients organized around relevant events, with explicit coverage and substitutes only where useful. An operational owner can be separate from email recipients.

What changing this would involve

The current contact list has no categories or TO/CC distinction. Adding them changes data, forms, validation and recipient resolution; it is not just a Default checkbox.

Questions to resolve

  • Is a single accountable person needed, or several recipients?
  • Are projects@ or billing@ mailboxes used? Must recipients have portal access?
Pending validation. No implementation authorized.
04Assigned PM versus subsample contactsMeaning to validate · Impact: Medium / high

Current situation and evidence

A subsample inherits a contact list or uses a custom list. The references describe a survey-specific Assigned PM ahead of default Director and PM contacts in recipient resolution.

Reference: S3 · S4 · P2. See the sources section.

What to challenge

The person responsible for the work and the email recipient are not necessarily the same. The references do not establish whether suppliers can edit that assignment.

Intermediate approach

Do not replace the current list with one person. Validate whether a separate owner is useful and who can assign them.

Ideal direction

A visible operational owner, separate from communication recipients. Both have an explicit scope: project or subsample, without assuming another inheritance layer.

What changing this would involve

This may require a new assignment relationship and SampleOne data. Relabelling the existing list as Assigned PM would imply a false equivalence.

Questions to resolve

  • Is Assigned PM an accountable person or just a notification address?
  • Is it assigned by Ipsos, the supplier, or both? Does it apply to a project or subsample?
Pending validation. No implementation authorized.
05Director-before-PM notification priorityPriority to validate · Impact: Medium / high

Current situation and evidence

The references resolve Assigned PM, then default Project Director, then default Project Manager. The prototype neither executes that chain nor sends those emails.

Reference: S3 · S4 · P2. See the sources section.

What to challenge

The most senior contact is not necessarily the right recipient for operational updates. S4 initially suggests fallback prevents missed notifications, but its own chain can end without sending anything.

Intermediate approach

Request an event-to-recipient matrix. Explore a visible warning when required communications have no recipient coverage.

Ideal direction

Transparent event-specific resolution, showing effective recipients and configuration origin. Define delivery and escalation rules before automating them.

What changing this would involve

Displaying a warning is relatively limited. Recipient resolution, sending, traceability and delivery require backend support and verified rules.

Questions to resolve

  • Should Director take priority over PM for every event?
  • Which messages are mandatory, and how should missing recipients be reported?
Pending validation. No implementation authorized.
06Finance, Sales and billing communicationsDocument inconsistency · Impact: Medium / high

Current situation and evidence

Both references describe Finance → Sales → PM for some billing emails, but also state that Finance is outside notification flows. Other cost events use Director/PM as TO and Sales as CC.

Reference: S3 · S4. See the sources section.

What to challenge

The resolver used by each event is not identified. Always copying Sales may be a current business need or a historical distribution rule.

Intermediate approach

Do not adopt either chain automatically. Complete an event, TO, CC, fallback and address-maintenance ownership matrix.

Ideal direction

Recipients based on communication needs, independently of commercial job titles. Keep mandatory copies only where there is a validated reason.

What changing this would involve

This changes distribution and integration rules, rather than merely adding Finance to a user selector. Do not infer an email address from a billing entity label.

Questions to resolve

  • Which messages does Finance actually receive?
  • When must Sales be copied? What does Billing entity mean as a recipient, and who maintains its address?
Pending validation. No implementation authorized.
07Who can approve costsCritical authorization question · Impact: High

Current situation and evidence

Only Administrator can approve or reject costs in the prototype. S4 says any user who can access the screen can approve. S3 says the stored procedure does not check roles and refers to an upstream access-control layer.

Reference: S3 · S4 · P3. See the sources section.

What to challenge

A missing stored-procedure check does not prove that the overall flow has no authorization rule. The Finance role alone also does not establish approval authority.

Intermediate approach

Keep the current restriction until the permission matrix is confirmed. Explore Approve costs as a separate delegation only if users need it.

Ideal direction

An explicit cost-decision capability with a defined scope and a record of who grants it. Team administration, billing notifications and approval remain separate responsibilities.

What changing this would involve

Authorization must be consistent across layers, with an explicit migration plan. Do not broaden access as part of a visual update.

Questions to resolve

  • Who approves costs in practice, and under what authorization?
  • Does approval authority cover the supplier or specific projects? Who grants and revokes it?
Pending validation. No implementation authorized.
08Confirming, rejecting and finalizing costsBusiness flow to validate · Impact: High

Current situation and evidence

The prototype supports individual and bulk Approve/Reject decisions, recording actor and time. S4 describes irreversible confirmation after CPI is locked, without explaining rejection or dispute handling.

Reference: S3 · S4 · P3. See the sources section.

What to challenge

It is unclear whether approval is a final acceptance or an acknowledgment of review. Reject may not reflect the actual process for a discrepancy.

Intermediate approach

Preserve the current flow during validation. Explore Request review as an alternative to Reject without renaming actions or changing states yet.

Ideal direction

Separate Ipsos publication, supplier review/dispute and final confirmation. Define reasons, ownership and resolution for discrepancies. Bulk actions depend on eligibility.

What changing this would involve

The current decision mutation also changes ipsosStatus and publishedAt. Validate and separate publication from confirmation before formalizing the flow. This is a prototype observation, not a rule stated in the HTML references.

Questions to resolve

  • Can acceptance be corrected, and by whom?
  • Is the real action rejection or a review request? What happens to a bulk selection containing an unlocked CPI?
Pending validation. No implementation authorized.
09Help Desk and Tech SupportActual usage to verify · Impact: Low to omit / high if accounts migrate

Current situation and evidence

Neither appears in the prototype permission catalogue. The references describe Help Desk as a label without active behavior and Tech Support as incompletely implemented.

Reference: S3 · S4. See the sources section.

What to challenge

Presenting them as active roles suggests behavior that the references do not demonstrate. Existing accounts or external processes might still depend on that data.

Intermediate approach

Do not introduce them yet. Investigate whether a support contact without special permissions is sufficient. Never deactivate or reassign existing accounts automatically.

Ideal direction

Only retain concepts with a verified purpose. Represent support as contact information or a concrete capability, according to need, without copying legacy defects.

What changing this would involve

Omitting them from a new catalogue is simple; removing their real-system equivalents requires an account inventory and operational validation.

Questions to resolve

  • Who consults these contacts, and in which process?
  • What access should accounts currently holding only Tech Support retain?
Pending validation. No implementation authorized.
10Delegation and auditabilityPermission matrix pending · Impact: Medium / high

Current situation and evidence

The prototype blocks self-deactivation and removal of the current user’s own Administrator role. The references do not detail which roles each team manager may grant.

Reference: S3 · S4 · P1. See the sources section.

What to challenge

Editing a contact should not automatically grant authority to appoint Administrators, authorize cost approval or change financial recipients.

Intermediate approach

Preserve current safeguards. Define who may administer users, permissions and recipients before expanding team management.

Ideal direction

Trace relevant access, contact and decision changes where required. Protect against leaving an account without administration, with verified recovery rules.

What changing this would involve

Access/contact audit history is not implemented. It needs persistent records, visibility rules and retention requirements, rather than an illustrative history screen alone.

Questions to resolve

  • Who appoints Administrators and changes billing recipients?
  • Is audit history required? Who may view it, and how is an account without an Administrator recovered?
Pending validation. No implementation authorized.
11Parent/child suppliersOut of scope for now · Impact: High

Current situation and evidence

The prototype associates each user with one supplier. Role references describe notification fallback to the parent; S2 says Redirects and S2S values do not automatically inherit or copy between suppliers.

Reference: S2 · S3 · S4 · P4. See the sources section.

What to challenge

These are different rules, not a direct contradiction. Presenting them as one inheritance model would be confusing and could broaden cross-account access.

Intermediate approach

Keep this outside implementation. Document cross-account access, contact fallback and integration configuration as separate concerns.

Ideal direction

Explicit account scope and configuration origin. A supplier family does not itself grant access or share credentials.

What changing this would involve

This affects identity, membership, search, reports, contacts and mutations. Do not address it with an account picker before validating the model.

Questions to resolve

  • Does the parent administer child users or only contacts?
  • Can the child see or disable fallback? Can one user work for several supplier accounts?
Pending validation. No implementation authorized.

Current situation and evidence

The documents do not explain a transition from Administrator/User and email lists to combined roles and role-specific defaults.

Reference: S3 · S4 · P1 · P2. See the sources section.

What to challenge

User is not automatically PM. Administrator is not a universal notification recipient. A group of email addresses cannot be reduced to one default without checking for losses.

Intermediate approach

Inventory users and recipients. Explore alternatives with fictional data, preserving current assignments until an authorized owner confirms each change.

Ideal direction

A reviewable migration with proposed assignments, access/recipient differences, human validation and correction before activation.

What changing this would involve

  • How are communications and access preserved during the transition?

Cross-app opportunities

  • Visible effective configuration: reuse defaults/custom language to explain recipients, without assuming contacts, integrations and child suppliers share the same rules.
  • Progressive contextual help: short notes for essential decisions and popovers for longer explanations. Keep internal implementation details and unresolved product questions out of final user-facing copy.
  • Understandable states: Finished does not guarantee a file is available; publishing costs is not confirming them; saved configuration is not proof of notification delivery.
  • Action-based authorization: the purpose of each permission should be explainable. Do not use Administrator as the default answer to every sensitive operation.
  • Agreed sources of truth: production catalogues, permissions and defaults require Engineering agreement. Legacy code and reference HTMLs are evidence to assess, not mandatory design decisions.

How to validate the intermediate approach with users

Use representative situations: one person with several responsibilities, a team with separate operational and finance contacts, and a team relying on shared mailboxes. Agree the research plan before committing to an interview count.

  1. Ask participants to identify who receives a subsample-opened notification and a costs-published notification.
  2. Ask what changes when the primary person is absent or leaves the organization.
  3. Compare the current list with the Project/Billing concept and observe which makes distribution easier to explain.
  4. Ask who may add a user and who may accept costs. Do not suggest that the two permissions should coincide.
  5. Walk through a cost discrepancy and a missing-recipient situation. Identify the expected action and feedback.

An alternative is useful if participants can anticipate who receives information and who may act without knowing internal role names or hidden priority rules.
